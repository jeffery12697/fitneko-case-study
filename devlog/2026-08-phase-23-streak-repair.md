# 2026-08 — Phase 23 closeout: selling streak repairs without selling streaks

*Phase 23's one unfinished item was "streak protection push notifications." The closeout decision was to not build it — and to build the opposite instead: no notification, no freeze item, just the ability to buy a broken streak back after the fact, priced so that honesty is load-bearing.*

## The problem

A streak feature without any protection mechanism punishes exactly the users it's supposed to retain: miss one day to a flight or a fever and weeks of momentum reset to zero. The obvious industry answers were both wrong for this product. A reminder push would burn the LINE free tier's 200 pushes/month on the one population that currently generates zero scheduled pushes — free users — and free users *are* the streak population, since streaks reward logging and logging is the free half of the product. A Duolingo-style pre-purchased freeze means storing a held-item state, auto-consumption logic, and credits spent on protection that may never trigger.

Repair-after-the-fact has neither problem. Its trigger moment is the user coming back — which is a reply, not a push, and "reply, don't push" has been this product's principle since the phase was specced.

## Decisions

**The price curve is the policy.** Repairing a gap of N days costs 1+2+…+N credits: one day is cheap (1 can), five days costs 15 — the entire monthly earning ceiling. That single curve replaces a whole knob I never had to build ("how far back can you repair?"): older breaks imply longer gaps, and longer gaps price themselves out. A hard cap of five days exists on top, but for interface reasons, not economic ones — the price stops the *purchase*, but without a cap the UI would still cheerfully *offer* a 188-day gap for 17,766 credits to someone returning after half a year. The cap value falls exactly on the affordability boundary (five days = the most anyone can earn in a month), so it only removes offers that were already dead.

**Achievements never count repaired days.** Streak achievements pay out credits, and repairs cost credits — an obvious loop: buy days, unlock the 30-day achievement, collect the payout. The loop has exactly one breakpoint and it's this rule: the display streak includes bought days, the achievement streak never does. Internally that means two streak computations living side by side, which is the design's one real tax — every call site had to be sorted into "display" or "truth," and a regression test pins the achievement side so nobody ever "simplifies" the two back into one. The UI puts the discrepancy on the table rather than hiding it: big number says 15 days, small print says 12 real + 3 repaired, and the achievement progress bar states it only counts real days.

**The calendar marks bought days as bought — and that's a mechanism, not a style choice.** I decided against a repair-count limit (someone could log every other day, repairing each 1-day gap for 1 can) because two structural gates already drain the exploit of any payoff: achievements ignore repaired days, so there's no material reward; and the calendar renders repaired days with a distinct canned-food icon — deliberately *not* a differently-colored flame, which after a week of looking at it would read as "fire I earned." Someone gaming repairs spends real earned credits to own a calendar that visibly says they gamed it. The spec records this as a design invariant: the visual distinction may never be removed for aesthetic reasons, because it's doing economic work.

**The repair request has an empty body.** The server computes the one repairable gap — the gap immediately before the current streak — and the client can't specify dates at all. That kills the arbitrary-history-repair privilege escalation and the race where two requests fight over the same gap, in the same stroke. Repairing a gap can expose the *next* older gap for purchase, chaining backwards; that's accepted, because every step genuinely extends the streak, genuinely costs credits, and stays visible on the calendar — and the length cap bounds the chain naturally. This endpoint is also a first: nothing in the app's API layer had ever deducted credits before (all spending lived in the chat pipeline), so the deduct-write-refund compensation pattern got its first port to the request/response world.

**The calendar sees past the free tier's window, but can't open it.** Free accounts have a 30-day history window. The streak calendar deliberately ignores it — it shows *that* you logged on a day, never *what* — because the window protects the content of records, not their existence, and a streak calendar that forgot your history would be lying about the very thing it exists to show. The boundary is pinned by tests from both directions: days outside the window render but aren't clickable through to detail (that would be a paywall backdoor), and a test asserts the calendar keeps ignoring the window (so nobody "consistently" clamps it later).

## Hindsight, honestly

- **It's a pressure valve.** The whole feature exists so users don't have to feel crushed by keeping the record alive every single day — miss a few days in the middle and the credits can buy them back.
